## 1. Purpose

Arro is a shopper-first, UCP-native commerce intelligence, trust, continuity, and
action product. Its first-party Expo application provides search, category,
product, comparison, saved, cart, checkout, native-payment, receipt, and account
surfaces. The same commerce core is also available through HTTP and MCP so
authorized integrations and agents do not need a parallel model.

The launch is not API/MCP-only. UCP-compatible business capabilities remain the
primary path for cart, checkout, order, identity, fulfillment, and business
actions. Approved read-only sources may support discovery, matching, comparison,
and public facts, but they do not create commerce-action authority. Unsupported
businesses receive explicit alternatives or handoff states; they do not gain
scraping, proxy, or browser-automation authority.

This brief defines Arro's non-negotiable scope, trust boundaries, architecture, and implementation sequence. Detailed specs for UCP integration, product intelligence, commerce flows, privacy, operations, and agentic commerce must inherit from it.

Reference hierarchy: this file controls current V1 product scope. Prior first-iteration material and external landscape research were used only to recover useful operating mechanics, trust UX, data-domain lessons, market signals, and post-V1 surface ideas that are deliberately copied into this brief. Research notes do not widen V1 unless a requirement is deliberately copied into this source-of-truth document.

The launch cut must answer one question with evidence: can an ordinary shopper
discover, compare, choose, review, and complete one supported merchant purchase
with less friction while Arro preserves source and merchant truth? HTTP/MCP
integration remains valuable, but it is not the launch acceptance proxy for the
first-party application.

## 2. Product Position

Arro's launch requirements are enforceable shared-core behaviors: fast
supported-source retrieval, source-labeled accuracy, current freshness state,
capability-aware actions, resumable commerce state, native-first checkout, and
less repeated context entry. Enforcement covers first-party UI, API/MCP payloads,
merchant responses, documentation, and support/audit outcomes. It does not mean
browser scraping or surveillance of third-party UI. No surface may introduce
paid-placement bias, unsupported claims, or unclear provenance.

The product is a commerce intelligence, trust, and action layer, not a generic marketplace or connector directory: it turns agent/user intent, structured preferences, typed memory, and explicit context into source-backed products, decision artifacts, and merchant-safe buying paths. Broad catalog discovery is valuable only when it feeds product detail, variant selection, comparison, typed decision state, cart preparation, checkout handoff, or gated completion without inventing authority that the source did not grant.

Category boundary: Arro is the shopper-facing commerce intelligence and
continuity layer between intent, source authority, merchant checkout, and payment
consent. Arro owns its first-party user surface and state orchestration; it does
not become the merchant, payment processor, wallet, browser, marketplace, or
owner of every evidence source.

Adjacent vertical transaction systems, such as restaurant ordering, travel booking, local services, social or app-directory commerce, and niche aggregators, are benchmarks and future distribution or source candidates only when they enter through the same source-governed contract. They do not widen the MLP source family or create V1 launch-visible authority by themselves, and their booking, order, shipping, fulfillment, availability, or payment terms must be treated as source-owned, buyer-context-specific facts that are revalidated before handoff or completion. Source-liquidity evidence means live endpoints or approved connectors, namespace and schema authority where applicable, negotiated capabilities, source freshness, conformance, data-use rights, reliability, and source-required identity or attestation state. Vertical charters, waitlists, app-directory listings, integration catalogs, tool or skill marketplace listings, official or featured badges, install counts, "try now" links, partner announcements, and public readiness badges are distribution-liquidity or public-readiness evidence only; they may inform outreach, compatibility testing, packaging, or future source review, but they do not become source, routing, or launch authority until Arro verifies the underlying source path and permitted data use.

The durable product unit is portable buying state, not a product card, chat
answer, connector response, or checkout button. Arro makes a shopping decision
resumable, refreshable, challengeable, handoff-ready, and completion-ready across
the current first-party app and approved integrations without letting any
surface invent source, ranking, or payment authority.

Strategic shorthand for the long-term direction: Arro acts like a super-app for
ecommerce. The first-party web/native application now proves that direction on
top of one shared commerce core; extensions, messaging, wearables, and other
channels remain later clients. The super-app is made possible by source
authority, typed state, merchant Orders, handoff quality, native checkout, and
continuity—not by becoming Merchant of Record, wallet, crawler, or marketplace.

Experience strategy: the first-party app renders trust, proof, policy,
source-authority, checkout, and receipt state as familiar commerce interactions.
Future messaging cards or channel views may reuse that state, but presentation
cannot create source, payment, checkout, ranking, wallet, or connector authority.

Experience design grammar: natural intent in, structured commerce state out, policy-gated action next, receipt continuity after. Future owned surfaces may use a stable intent-entry and continuity shell, such as voice, text, add/import, save, back, menu, and continue controls, around adaptive commerce surfaces. That shell is not the product moat and not a V1 dependency; it is useful only when it exposes Arro-owned buying state. A future Arro command home should therefore center active receipts, pending approvals, stale decisions, price or stock changes, checkout attempts, order or recovery states, and agent permissions, not a generic product feed or shopping search destination.

Arro's durable user-value pillars are confidence, savings, continuity, authority, convenience, independence, and protection. Every V1 feature should map to at least one of these values through source-backed answers, better alternatives, preserved decision context, explicit authority state, reduced repeated work, commercial independence, or no-buy warnings.

User-facing and agent-summary behavior should reuse familiar shopping, checkout, and developer-platform conventions wherever possible. Arro can be novel in verification, routing, memory, no-buy warnings, and policy enforcement, but the visible task flow should still feel like search, compare, save, review cart, confirm purchase, continue to merchant, or check order status. Internal primitives stay useful because they make familiar commerce actions safer, not because users must learn a new commerce language.

Simplicity is a product constraint, not a polish layer. Every first-party screen,
API response, MCP result, and host-renderable summary exposes the smallest
truthful shopping state: what was checked, what is current or estimated, why it
matters, what is blocked, and the next safe action. Advanced machinery stays in
developer and audit paths; shoppers do not need protocol or policy vocabulary.

Launch distribution starts with Arro's first-party web/native app and can meet
users inside approved external surfaces through the same API/MCP core. Neither
path is allowed to fork product truth or checkout authority.

This is a timing and trust-distribution bet, not only a protocol bet. Host AI platforms, payment providers, marketplaces, and commerce platforms may vertically integrate parts of commerce intelligence over time. Arro's defensibility window depends on becoming the reliable commerce preflight and action-policy layer before those surfaces fully internalize product identity, comparison, no-buy reasoning, handoff quality, and source-liquidity work. Speed, trust-signal preservation agreements, partner trust, source depth, and reusable receipts matter as much as protocol compliance.

Keyless catalog starter kits, UCP examples, tool gateways, and agent-ready commerce demos make raw product search increasingly easy to copy. Arro should treat that as the minimum integration-speed bar, not the moat. The differentiator is what happens after first catalog access: source labels that survive host rendering, seller and variant diligence, product-detail and comparison quality, no-buy or safer-next-action reasoning, portable `DecisionReceipt` state, explicit `ActionPolicy`, and safe continuation or gated completion without leaking source, checkout, wallet, ranking, or payment authority.

Arro must work across platform-owned, merchant-owned, and user-owned agent surfaces without letting agent ownership or funding alter Arro's source-backed result. Host funding, merchant control, ads, preferred placement, or platform monetization cannot change Arro's source labels, recommendation basis, caveats, no-buy warnings, action limits, or required disclosures.

The first-party shopping destination is a launch requirement. Hosted identity,
data-rights, privacy, security-notice, and support-recovery surfaces remain part
of the same product boundary and may not become a wallet, marketplace, or source
of merchant authority.

Plain-language strategy answer:

- What Arro does: Arro helps people discover, compare, save, review, and buy from
  source-labeled merchant facts in its first-party app, while offering the same
  guarded commerce capabilities to approved integrations and agents.
- Who needs Arro: users need confidence, savings, continuity, authority, convenience, independence, and protection; agents need reliable tools, source labels, authority state, and safe action boundaries; businesses need high-intent demand, correct representation, preserved checkout and loyalty control, attribution, fewer bad handoffs, and lower return or support risk.
- What Arro is not: Arro is not a connector directory, crawler, generic aggregator, marketplace, ad network, merchant dashboard, wallet, or checkout owner. Connectors provide authority; Arro's value is the trust, routing, normalization, memory, policy, evaluation, and action layer across many sources and agent surfaces.
- Why agents call Arro: agents cannot safely infer live commerce truth, checkout authority, variant correctness, return risk, payment readiness, or merchant support state from generic web text. Arro gives them a typed commerce result they can cite, continue, hand off, or complete under policy.
- Why users trust Arro: Arro does not sell the recommendation, hides weak matches, explains uncertainty, preserves useful decision context, asks for confirmation before sensitive actions, and treats "do not buy" as successful value.
- Why businesses want or accept Arro: Arro sends high-intent demand while preserving merchant-of-record control, checkout control, brand, offer, loyalty, source freshness, payment-handler boundaries, customer support paths, and approved attribution instead of scraping, impersonating users, or replacing checkout.
- How Arro becomes hard to replace: the compounding assets are not a UI shell; they are source liquidity, intent liquidity, product identity, typed decision memory, policy gates, decision receipts, no-buy metrics, conformance evidence, partner trust, and agent distribution packaging. These assets matter only when approved agents can render them simply, users can feel the protection, and partners can rely on the same allowed-next-action contract across surfaces. Treat easily copied packaging or single-host integrations as distribution wins, not durable moats, unless they feed one of these compounding assets.

Ownership boundaries:

- Arro owns the commerce trust layer: intent normalization, source labels, product identity, comparison logic, typed decision memory, action policy, decision receipts, no-buy warnings, commercial isolation, handoff quality, gated completion checks, agent harness evidence, and agent distribution packaging.
- UCP-compatible businesses, official feeds, and approved connectors own source authority: product facts, capability grants, cart and checkout state, payment-handler support, order evidence, support paths, and merchant-of-record control.
- External agents, model runtimes, messaging tools, browser agents, realtime or voice hosts, OS surfaces, and future Arro-owned AI surfaces own user entry and presentation. Inside Arro, they do not create merchant authority, alter Arro ranking, bypass confirmation, or reach Arro-managed merchant connectors outside policy gates.

Core V1 product primitives and trust packaging:

- `IntentRecord` / `DecisionReceipt`: the private intent record plus portable, host-citable decision artifact pair that records user intent, constraints, sources, alternatives, caveats, commercial disclosures, recommendation or no-buy warning, action authority, handoff state, completion state where supported, and what could go wrong before the next action. This pair is the typed buying-state artifact that lets an approved surface resume, refresh, challenge, hand off, or complete the same commerce decision under policy instead of re-summarizing it from scratch. For high-uncertainty, no-buy, cart, handoff, and gated completion flows, the receipt should preserve an internal decision-quality trail: required commerce checks completed, source-backed facts, unresolved gaps, strongest fit and risk reasons, challenged assumptions, rejected alternatives, and why the next action is allowed, limited, blocked, or safer as handoff.
- `ActionPolicy`: the authority model that separates read, compare, remember, refresh, cart-preparing, handoff-generating, checkout-impacting, gated `complete_checkout`, and unbounded payment-executing states.
- `Source Liquidity Map`: internal routing infrastructure that tracks which approved sources, UCP endpoints, capability namespaces, feeds, evidence sources, and tools can answer or act with the required authority, freshness, schema-origin binding, schema and version compatibility, conformance evidence, confidence, latency, reliability, cost, rights, permission, source-required agent identity or attestation state, and capability-confidence state.
- `Intent Liquidity Map`: internal demand-routing infrastructure that tracks which agent surfaces, integrations, prompts, categories, channels, and contexts create high-intent shopping tasks, without exposing individual user-intent feeds or selling influence.
- `Arro Cards` / presentation contract: the stable renderable layer over canonical API and MCP responses. In the MLP this begins as one generic `commerce_card`; strategically it is the first primitive of intent-specific renderable commerce workflows, not decorative product tiles. Post-MLP expansion may add card atoms such as facts, source labels, warnings, metrics, trust badges, risk badges, receipt references, prices, availability, and actions; card families such as intent form, proof, source-state, product/source detail, comparison, sanity-check, decision-receipt, no-buy, checkout-gate, payment-handler-proof, continuation, completion, and recovery; and layouts such as single-card answers, carousels, comparison tables or boards, steppers, checklists or intake forms, approval gates, receipt timelines, and local or map-style surfaces where source data supports them. Cards are a visual and interactive expression of portable buying state, not a separate product surface, source of truth, ranking layer, checkout owner, or app dependency. Hosts and future first-party surfaces must route decisions from canonical structured fields such as source labels, freshness, caveats, authority limits, allowed next actions, receipt references, extracted intent fields, proof reasons, risk notes, and checkout or payment-handler state rather than from decorative card copy. Arro Workflows are allowed-next-action sequences governed by `ActionPolicy`, not LLM-generated follow-up prompts.
- Commerce Capability Manifest: the public and machine-readable package that explains Arro's safe capabilities, MCP/OpenAPI surfaces, permission matrix, sample decision receipts, unsafe actions, unsupported states, source labels, and commercial-independence promise.
- Agent Harness: the internal replay, conformance, policy, memory, receipt, observability, and manual-verification layer that proves the same source-governed commerce behavior across HTTP, MCP, host package mappings, and partner integrations. It is not a generic autonomous-agent runtime, and it cannot create source authority, checkout authority, ranking authority, or memory authority outside Arro's commerce contracts.
- Host verification claims, runtime completion contracts, multi-model deliberation, and background-agent summaries are useful integration evidence only when bound back to Arro-owned source checks, `DecisionReceipt` state, `ActionPolicy` state, and replay or verification artifacts. They never substitute for Arro harness evidence, final source revalidation, or user confirmation.

Headless V1 capabilities:

- Let authorized agents and integrations call Arro through HTTP APIs and one canonical Arro MCP server.
- Provide a documentation-led agent integration path: discover live capabilities, understand auth and scopes, install or call the MCP/API surface, inspect schemas and examples, run safe read-first examples, JSON-first tool-result examples, quote or receipt previews before execution, integration diagnostics, one narrow checkout-continuation or gated-completion example where approved, or clearly labeled demo payloads, and see failure examples without overclaiming merchant coverage, checkout authority, or production source access. The default first-success path should let a competent external developer run one read-first search/detail/receipt flow, one blocked or unavailable-state example, one diagnostics check, and one policy-gated continuation/completion path quickly without reading the full manifest, raw merchant schemas, or a long policy spec.
- Convert natural-language or structured shopping intent into catalog search requests, filters, constraints, comparisons, and source-grounded explanations.
- Return product details, variants, media, seller context, availability, warnings, and current price context as structured tool/API results.
- Compare current buying options, including new, resale, refurbished, open-box, or certified secondhand options only where compatible data passes launch gates.
- Preserve typed commerce memory, `IntentRecord` / `DecisionReceipt` artifacts, and decision state such as size, region, currency, budget, accepted alternatives, rejected options, saved product references, cart handoff references, completion references, and stale references. External memory providers, host memory, vector stores, user-modeling systems, and agent-session logs may assist retrieval or migration only through explicit typed adapters, consent, provenance, prompt-exposure classification, export/deletion controls, and Arro validation; they must not become the source of truth for product facts, ranking, checkout policy, payment state, or action authority.
- Return host-portable result shapes that downstream agents and future first-party surfaces can render as familiar product cards, proof cards, comparison summaries, warning panels, saved decisions, cart reviews, checkout confirmations, merchant handoffs, and order-status updates without inventing a new authority model or forcing users to learn Arro's internal primitives.
- Require approved integrations to preserve human-visible rendering when they present Arro output to a user, and agent-visible structured fields otherwise, for required source labels, freshness state, caveats, no-buy warnings, commercial disclosures, authority limits, and allowed next actions. Early preview or approved packages should include at least one human-readable receipt or no-buy rendering check, such as what was checked, why to wait or stop, and the next safe action, before they are used as proof that Arro's protection is perceptible without first-party shopping UI. If a host cannot render required trust signals faithfully, Arro must degrade the integration status, remove approved listing claims, return a safer handoff or blocked state, or require a Arro-controlled support/recovery surface where appropriate.
- Reduce dead ends through explicit unsupported, limited, stale, denied, or partner-required states, supported alternatives, and capability-preserving handoffs.
- Let an authorized agent prepare a business-scoped cart draft or checkout continuation where negotiated cart or checkout capabilities allow it, so merchant-hosted handoff feels like a prepared purchase path rather than a generic redirect.
- Reduce repeated checkout context entry through typed preferences, fulfillment hints, language, currency, and supported payment-handler readiness without storing raw payment credentials.
- Keep Arro accounts optional in V1; end users may later migrate an agent or guest session into a permanent Arro identity only by explicit choice.
- Support gated `complete_checkout` in V1 only where negotiated business capabilities, source-ready checkout status, explicit user step-up authorization, explicit budget or total-cost constraint pass, payment-handler proof, a Arro-issued server-bound attempt/idempotency key, rate limits, final source revalidation, and durable audit are all present. Hosted or merchant-controlled checkout handoff remains the default when any completion gate is missing.
- Support a headless product sanity-check pattern: from an integration-submitted URL string, product identifier, user-submitted text, or structured claim set, produce a source-labeled `DecisionReceipt` with verified facts, weak or stale claims, alternatives, return or variant risk, no-buy warnings where justified, and the safest next action. V1 must not fetch, scrape, screenshot, proxy, crawl, or automate unsupported pages to power this pattern.
- Track no-buy value in V1 metrics as a precision-reviewed trust signal, not a raw count to maximize: bad alternatives rejected, wrong variants prevented, stale claims corrected, failed completion gates caught, return-risk warnings shown, and unsupported checkout attempts blocked. Automated evaluation sets, drift alerts, sampled receipts, labeled holdout cases, and commercial-pressure scenarios feed a monthly launch-ramp review by product and data owners, so caveats do not drift into hidden ranking manipulation or get suppressed under revenue pressure without forcing exhaustive item-by-item human review.

Minimum Lovable Product release cut:

- Keep the MLP read/receipt-led, but not advice-only. It includes one canonical HTTP path and one canonical MCP path for source-backed search, product detail, source state, simple comparison, product sanity-check, diagnostics, minimal `IntentRecord` / `DecisionReceipt` creation, prepared `continue_url` fallback, and one tightly gated `complete_checkout` proof for a supported business-scoped source path.
- Keep the exposed MLP tool surface narrower than the broader V1 schema vocabulary. Broader V1 tool names may exist in contracts, manifests, or internal schemas, but a pilot host may receive only the explicitly verified MLP allowlist for that host package; limited, internal, or broader V1 tools stay unlisted, disabled, or blocked until their source, policy, idempotency, rendering, and audit gates pass.
- Make the first lovable moment a human-readable receipt, no-buy warning, prepared checkout continuation, or gated completion result that a host can show plainly: what Arro checked, what is current or uncertain, why to buy, wait, compare, continue securely, complete, or stop, and the next safe action.
- Keep MLP presentation intentionally narrow. The MLP may expose one `structuredContent.presentation` / `commerce_card` carrier that renders proof, no-buy, comparison, receipt, checkout-gate, payment-handler-proof, continuation, completion, or recovery states where the canonical response supports them; adaptive layout systems, editable intent forms, maps, carousels, first-party command homes, visual workflow builders, and multi-item purchase-plan UIs are post-MLP unless a source-of-truth review explicitly scopes them. - Make the MLP first-success path competitive with raw catalog starter kits: a competent external agent developer should reach a live source-labeled search/detail call quickly from the docs and examples, then immediately see the extra Arro layer through source-state diagnostics, comparison or sanity-check behavior, no-buy or safer-next-action output, a portable receipt, and the narrow continuation/completion contract. MLP preview Agent Distribution Kit means only minimal manifests, canonical MCP/OpenAPI metadata, one quickstart, one safe or blocked example, one receipt example, diagnostics, host-side tool allowlist instructions, and pilot evidence instructions. The tool allowlist limits local host exposure; it is not prior Arro approval for an authenticated agent using exact portable payment capability. Full Agent Distribution Kit, registry/app-directory submissions, broad listing copy, paid-tool metadata, managed-channel adapters, channel pilots, host-specific production packages, toolkit sync, and broad partner packaging are post-MLP.
- First MLP target pair: Shopify is the first source family where official Storefront Catalog MCP, guarded Shopify Global Catalog, or legacy Storefront GraphQL can pass source-governance and safe-URL gates; Hermes Agent is the first named external agent surface over Arro's canonical MCP/HTTP contracts. Neither target receives special source, ranking, wallet, memory, checkout, or payment authority outside the same gates as every other source or host. Any pilot use of legacy Storefront GraphQL must carry a named review date, owner, compatibility-bridge evidence label, and removal trigger in the pilot tracker or operating contract.
- Keep monetization invisible in the MLP. It may record approved attribution or usage internally where already safe, but the MLP is evaluated by agent calls, successful first integration, human-visible receipt/no-buy rendering, useful no-buy or correction outcomes, successful prepared continuation, safe gated completion behavior, blocked unsafe attempts, and repeat agent use, not GMV optimization or checkout conversion.
- Do not treat the MLP as a demo shell. Every MLP path must use real source governance, real contracts, real policy gates, real audit, real continuation/fallback behavior, real idempotency, and real failure states. Demo payloads are allowed only as labeled examples, not as launch evidence.
- The MLP succeeds when an external agent can integrate quickly, call Arro on a real shopping question, render the required trust signals, produce at least one shopper-perceptible moment of confidence, correction, no-buy value, or safer next action, and prove one business-scoped checkout path can either complete after explicit authorization or degrade to a prepared `continue_url` without first-party shopping UI. At least one pilot scenario must show value a host would not get by calling the first source directly: a cross-source or source-gap comparison, stale-claim or wrong-variant catch, no-buy warning with plain reasons, safer-next-action decision, or portable `DecisionReceipt` that preserves source authority and action state across calls.
- Maintain a short MLP operating contract or pilot tracker alongside this brief for daily execution. It must list exposed tools, allowed states, required receipt fields, blocked states, source gates, completion gates, pilot evidence, post-MLP exclusions, exception records, review due date, and simplification trigger. If the named MLP pilot review window closes without a real external-agent call, host-rendered trust signal, and shopper-perceptible receipt, no-buy, safer-next-action, continuation, or gated-completion proof, the next action is simplification of the MLP path rather than expansion of features, sources, surfaces, or gates.

Business and platform requirements:

- Send supported businesses high-intent discovery, comparison, cart, checkout, and re-engagement traffic without paid placement, pay-to-rank, or misrepresented product facts.
- Treat connector breadth as an authority input, not the product moat. The moat is the source-governed decision and action layer that makes many connectors, feeds, agent surfaces, and handoff paths safe and comparable.
- Keep Arro monetization invisible to product guidance through the commercial-isolation and commercial-parity rules in this brief: commercial records stay internal and cannot affect ranking, recommendations, coupons, checkout totals, AI guidance, safety, or basic supported discovery.
- Primary V1 adoption and revenue thesis: hyperscale by making basic Arro trust feel free, native, or already covered by the user's chosen AI agent, host surface, or integration. Users and agents should not need a second subscription, checkout surcharge, or per-decision fee for source-labeled comparison, no-buy warnings, basic commerce preflight, safe handoff readiness, `DecisionReceipt`, or required checkout gates. Monetization stays behind the shopping moment through approved attribution, platform or integration partnerships, service-payment-backed tool access, capacity tiers, optional premium evidence, SLA, onboarding, support, and funding-supported growth while default-agent adoption is still the priority.
- Protect the free-basic-trust promise with caching, cost-class routing, abuse controls, integration-scoped fair-use limits, and useful-outcome budgets measured across receipts, no-buy warnings, safe handoffs, saves, corrections, source-gap learning, and gated completions. Do not throttle or price integrations by checkout conversion alone, because avoided bad purchases are part of Arro's user value.
- Maintain source-liquidity and conformance evidence for routing quality through capability-limited states, production-equivalent validation, and fallback behavior that keeps discovery, comparison, save, and supported-alternative flows usable while approval is pending.
- Improve conversion quality by preserving authoritative price, availability, fulfillment, coupon, disclosure, returnability, and checkout state before supported handoff or confirmation instead of sending users into stale, misleading, or contextless flows.
- Preserve business-controlled brand, offer, loyalty, membership, disclosure, payment-handler, support, and post-purchase rules when Arro or AI surfaces represent a business to a shopper.
- Treat shipping, fulfillment, direct-order, booking, market, buyer-region, B2B, POS, app-provided-rate, and order-channel state as business- or platform-owned capability facts, not static product facts inferred once during discovery.
- Improve agent and AI-surface discovery through complete source-backed product, offer, fulfillment, policy, return, review, support, loyalty, brand-fact, and public capability records rather than stale scraped pages or surface-specific buttons. This data quality is agent-readiness infrastructure, not paid media inventory; merchant enrichment, feed optimization, or campaign spend cannot override evidence, caveats, no-buy warnings, or user intent.
- Create approved affiliate or partner attribution where allowed, while keeping commercial records internal and isolated from ranking, recommendations, coupons, checkout totals, and AI guidance.
- Verify approved attribution survives the actual host-rendered handoff path, including `continue_url`, allowed referral parameters, server-to-server postbacks, affiliate-network callbacks, settlement feeds, or business reports where used. An integration or partner path cannot be treated as approved for attribution until end-to-end handoff and reconciliation evidence passes.
- Expose products to external AI shopping surfaces through source-labeled tool/API results, public platform capabilities, and stable API/MCP documentation after contracts and policy controls are ready.
- Operate safely inside ad-funded or commercially mediated AI surfaces without becoming ad-driven: host-surface ads, campaigns, or preferred placement cannot alter Arro ranking, Q&A, source labels, trust labels, memory, checkout totals, or no-buy warnings.
- Reduce support, dispute, and abandonment risk by keeping every purchase business-scoped, traceable, idempotent, and auditable.
- Do not build merchant-readiness dashboards, compliance dashboards, B2B SaaS analytics, or pay-to-improve scorecards as part of V1. Source quality exists to route user intent safely, not to sell influence.
- Package Arro as the safe commerce preflight call for external agents, browser agents, AI apps, and future channel surfaces: get structured facts, authority state, caveats, and handoff or gated completion readiness before a tool or agent attempts checkout-impacting work.
- Use aggregated unmet-demand, source-gap, blocked-action, and no-buy metrics for product and partner prioritization only. Do not create individual user-intent feeds, paid influence products, or dashboard-led B2B SaaS motions from those signals in V1.
- Preserve a public trust-charter promise in product language: Arro does not sell the recommendation. Commercial relationships may be disclosed, but they cannot change ranking, recommendation, Q&A, memory, trust labels, source-state text, checkout totals, or no-buy warnings.

V1 data requirement: current, verified commerce data takes priority over broad unverified coverage.

MLP differentiation is validated by working headless product capabilities, not positioning claims: external-agent access, current source-labeled signals, product detail quality, simple comparison quality, product sanity-check quality, visible `DecisionReceipt` or no-buy value, source governance, unsupported-state clarity, prepared `continue_url` fallback quality, gated completion safety, and quick integration must pass the MLP gates before they are used as public claims. Broader V1 claims such as full typed commerce memory, broad business-scoped cart preparation, broad checkout handoff, broad gated completion, gift support, multi-agent workflow support, secondhand/resale breadth, or reduced repeated checkout context wait for their own post-MLP launch gates.

Trust and agent-output requirements:

- Rank, recommend, and summarize from user intent, source evidence, freshness, policy, and risk fields only; commercial attribution and payout terms must stay out of shopper guidance.
- Do not expose ads, sponsored results, sponsored recommendations, paid editorial units, promoted products, or pay-to-rank modules through Arro-owned APIs, tools, or future first-party surfaces.
- Support search, product detail, comparison, source-state inspection, typed decision state, and cart-preparation readiness before requiring a permanent Arro account.
- Enforce only explicit budget and total-cost constraints supplied by the user, session, mandate, or authorized integration before cart preparation, checkout handoff, or gated checkout completion. Arro does not invent discretionary price limits, value judgments, or hidden risk-scoring at checkout; if final source-revalidated totals exceed the explicit constraint, fail closed or require renewed user authorization.
- Make saved and shared buying-decision artifacts resumable, explainable, and privacy-safe without exposing private checkout, payment, address, identity, or raw agent-memory data.
- Translate Arro's internal primitives into familiar shopping language in every user-facing or agent-summary output. Users should see "what Arro checked," "why this recommendation," "what can happen next," "review and confirm," or "continue to merchant," not internal artifact names or protocol states.
- Keep human-readable tool summaries plain: no raw scores, thresholds, protocol names, capability names, internal state keys, model metadata, schema terms, or error codes.
- Use UCP-first commerce paths and show explicit state labels when a business is unsupported, limited, pending, denied, stale, or unavailable.
- Keep multi-business shopping as an intelligent planning layer plus business-scoped carts and checkouts. Do not imply one atomic cart, one payment, one order, or one return flow across businesses.

Terminology and translation rules:

- A source label means the visible or stored fact includes source business or approved-source identity, fact type, fetched or validated timestamp, freshness expiry or TTL class, and whether the fact is advisory catalog data or authoritative cart, checkout, order, webhook, or settlement data.
- A plain match reason is a short user-facing explanation grounded in product identifiers, variant attributes, shared specs, condition, seller context, price, fulfillment, or returnability differences. It must not expose raw scores, model names, confidence thresholds, or ranking internals.
- Freshness metadata means source response hash where available, fetched-at, validated-at, expiry, refresh path, and binding status. Binding status must distinguish catalog hints from cart, checkout, payment, fulfillment, order, and webhook authority.
- Commercial isolation means ranking, search, recommendation, prompt, coupon, and checkout-total code paths cannot read payout terms, commission rates, settlement state, affiliate priority fields, or source-returned sponsored, boosted, campaign, promoted, paid direct-offer placement, or paid-placement metadata except through audited disclosure, attribution, reconciliation, or commercial-reporting jobs that cannot influence shopper guidance.
- Commercial parity means free, paid, host-paid, affiliate-covered, non-affiliate, service-payment-backed, licensed, and partner-integrated paths preserve the same source accuracy, safety gates, trust labels, no-buy warnings, and basic supported-product discovery. Paid tiers, usage fees, integration or platform licenses, or machine-payment rails may buy capacity, latency, higher tool budgets, integration support, SLA, onboarding, or optional premium evidence that is not required for safe basic decisioning; they must not buy or paywall ranking, source accuracy, required caveats, no-buy warnings, safe handoff readiness, required checkout gates, hidden eligibility, suppressed alternatives, or weaker checkout controls.
- Agent service-payment rails are host-, integration-, platform-, explicitly user-authorized, or Arro-funded mechanisms for API access, MCP tools, premium evidence, paid content, or provider-agent work. They are not shopper payment rails, merchant checkout, wallet custody, product-truth evidence, or authority to hide safety output. A service-payment receipt can prove that a service call was priced, authorized, paid, or settled; it does not prove that a product answer is correct unless Arro separately validates the answer through source governance.
- Payment-handler proof means provider-scoped evidence that the user authorized the specific checkout-impacting attempt, such as a passkey-approved mandate, signed payment intent, approval state or request ID, payment-handler session reference, one-time credential status, or equivalent provider confirmation. It is sensitive execution evidence, not product truth, merchant authority, wallet custody, ranking input, or permission to bypass source revalidation. Raw payment credentials, one-time card tokens, dynamic CVVs, cryptograms, private keys, and secrets must never be logged, stored in `IntentRecord` / `DecisionReceipt`, exposed to host agents, or used as recommendation inputs; only redacted references, status, scope, and audit links may be retained.
- Payment receipts, mandates, payment-handler proofs, and service-payment receipts prove authorization, settlement, provider state, or service-call status. Arro's `DecisionReceipt` proves commerce diligence: what was checked, which source-backed facts and gaps mattered, why a recommendation or no-buy warning was produced, what risks remain, and what action is allowed next.
- Host-agent compute delegation means Arro may, where a client protocol and host policy support it, ask the calling AI agent or host client to perform language-heavy, non-authoritative work such as intent extraction, clarification, summarization, subjective tradeoff framing, or low-risk argument repair. Delegated outputs are untrusted suggestions bound back to the original integration/session and user-intent evidence; they cannot materially change product, variant, quantity, seller, price ceiling, shipping or address scope, payment path, coupon choice, checkout action, or confirmation state without explicit user confirmation and Arro-owned validation. This can reduce duplicate model subscriptions and Arro inference cost, but cannot replace Arro-owned source validation, source labels, `ActionPolicy`, `DecisionReceipt`, handoff readiness, gated completion checks, Arro-issued server-bound checkout idempotency, audit, privacy controls, or final commerce authority.
- A merchant offer or loyalty perk is a source-backed commerce fact, not an ad, only when it is shown because it is relevant to the user's selected product, comparison, cart, checkout, linked entitlement, or explicit watch and is labeled with source, eligibility, conditions, expiry or freshness, and whether it is advisory or checkout-confirmed. It becomes an ad-like unit when placement, frequency, ranking, language, or targeting is driven by payment, campaign budget, merchant preference, or broad promotion unrelated to the user's active shopping task. If an approved or connected source returns sponsored, boosted, campaign, promoted, paid direct-offer placement, or paid-placement metadata inside a catalog or offer response, Arro must classify that commercial metadata separately from source-backed offer terms, isolate it from ranking and recommendation inputs, preserve disclosure where required, and verify through harness coverage that shopper guidance remains unchanged by the commercial flag.
- Unsupported fallback is a discovery and matching mode for user-submitted signals or approved read-only sources when the requested business action lacks approved UCP authority. It must not create cart, checkout, order, identity, payment, coupon, fulfillment, or other business-action capability.
- An `IntentRecord` is the private, typed record of what the user or authorized agent is trying to accomplish, including constraints, budget or total-cost rules, accepted tradeoffs, rejected options, source permissions, and required confirmation state.
- A `DecisionReceipt` is Arro's portable agent-facing product primitive and audit record: what was checked, which sources were used, why a recommendation or no-buy warning was produced, what caveats apply, what commercial disclosure exists, what evidence gaps or challenged assumptions remain, and what action is allowed next.
- An `ActionPolicy` is the compiled permission state for a request, session, source, tool, and user authorization. It must distinguish advisory read/compare/memory actions from cart-preparing, handoff-generating, checkout-impacting, gated `complete_checkout`, and unbounded payment-executing actions.
- A `Source Liquidity Map` is internal routing infrastructure. It may inform source selection, fallback, freshness warnings, conformance work, and capability-confidence rules, but it is not a merchant-facing scorecard, paid ranking surface, or compliance product.
- An `Intent Liquidity Map` is internal demand-routing infrastructure. It may inform launch categories, agent-surface priority, integration examples, source-gap prioritization, and partner sequencing, but it is not a user-intent marketplace, retargeting feed, ad product, or merchant bidding surface.
- A Commerce Capability Manifest is Arro's canonical public trust contract for agent ecosystems. It describes supported HTTP and MCP capabilities, auth and consent rules, allowed and unsafe actions, action-policy levels, sample receipts, source-state meanings, rate limits, commercial-isolation commitments, trust-signal preservation expectations, and integration examples. It does not certify merchant coverage or authorize any action by itself. The Agent Distribution Kit wraps this manifest with docs, examples, registry copy, install guidance, and integration materials; it must not create a second conflicting capability contract.
- A product sanity-check flow is a DecisionReceipt-producing pattern that tests a product, claim, link string, social reference, or submitted context against approved sources. It is valuable for users and agents, but it remains advisory unless UCP or an approved source provides commerce authority.
- A connector is a source-authority path into a business, platform, feed, or approved evidence source. It is not the user product, not the agent product, and not an independent checkout right. Arro's product starts when connector output becomes source-labeled product intelligence, action policy, decision memory, handoff, or gated completion.
- A prepared checkout continuation is a checkout handoff where Arro preserves the selected product, variant, cart or quote context, source labels, caveats, expiry, and next action in its own payload and session state so the user can continue with less repeated work where the merchant-hosted checkout honors the context. It is not a guarantee that the merchant page will never expire, drop state, or ask for additional buyer input; Arro must detect recoverable context loss where possible and degrade to refresh, retry, or blocked state.
- Familiar-commerce output means user-facing copy and host-renderable fields follow standard shopping verbs and states such as search, compare, save, cart, checkout, review, confirm, continue to merchant, order status, return, support, unavailable, out of stock, price changed, and requires sign-in. Familiar output does not expose Arro's internal primitive names except in developer documentation, schemas, audit records, or explicitly technical API responses.
- A simple agent contract means approved agents receive stable structured fields for result type, source label, freshness, plain reason, caveat or risk, blocked reason, allowed next action, handoff or completion state, and receipt reference instead of needing to infer commerce policy from prose.
- Internal states such as `unknown`, `profile_fetched`, `partner_required`, `limited`, `rate_limited`, `denied`, `error`, and `capability-limited` are implementation keys. User-facing copy must map them to plain status, reason, and next-action text without exposing the raw key.

## 5. Headless and Future Surface Experience Requirements

V1 implements these requirements as structured HTTP responses and MCP tool results. First-party web, mobile, extension, public pages, notifications, visual capture, and channel surfaces are post-V1 consumers of the same contracts unless explicitly reopened by scope review.

### Product Detail Tool Result and Future Product Page

Every product detail result or future product detail page must answer:

- What is this product?
- Which variants are available?
- What is the current price or price range?
- Is the item available?
- What media, specifications, ratings, warnings, and seller details are available?
- Is the offer new, resale, refurbished, open-box, or certified secondhand, and what condition, authenticity, returnability, and freshness signals are available?
- Which disclosures or warnings are advisory, blocking, require acknowledgement, or require buyer action according to the business-provided data?
- Can this item be saved, compared, added to cart, or checked out?
- What shipping, delivery, or pickup options are currently available where negotiated and supported?
- What is current, what may change, and what will be final only at checkout?

### Capability-Limited Product States

Product detail results and future product pages preserve available actions and clear limitations when a business exposes only part of the target UCP surface.

Required behavior:

- Map access policy and negotiated capabilities into explicit action states: available, unavailable, requires handoff, requires account, partner pending, rate-limited, denied, unsupported, or temporarily stale.
- Treat merchant-managed agentic storefront settings, Catalog access, direct-checkout settings, access tier, and channel eligibility as capability state. Do not present a channel opt-out, direct-checkout opt-out, low access tier, or rate limit as product unavailability.
- Keep internal state names out of user-facing copy. Translate raw labels such as `partner_required`, `denied`, or `error` into plain action explanations.
- Catalog-only products can be inspected, saved as typed decision state, compared, and refreshed, but cart, checkout, coupon, fulfillment, and order actions are disabled or routed to supported alternatives.
- Catalog plus cart products can support basket building with estimated totals, but checkout is not promised until checkout is negotiated and current.
- Checkout-capable products revalidate price, availability, fulfillment, discounts, disclosures, payment handlers, status, and messages before the user confirms. If the checkout requires buyer input, buyer review, escalation, hosted handoff, or missing context that Arro cannot safely collect, return the handoff and reason instead of trying to complete the purchase.
- Treat checkout handoff quality as a measured product outcome: the handoff should qualify as a prepared checkout continuation, and Arro should track whether the user can continue without repeating the shopping decision.
- If discount capability is missing, Arro may record the user's attempted code but must clearly state that the business did not validate it.
- `partner_required`, `denied`, `rate_limited`, and unsupported states are not presented as product unavailability. Explain the access state separately from the item's business-provided availability.
- Limited or unsupported states offer supported alternatives only when internal match-quality thresholds are met and preserve the user's saved product or comparison context.
- Do not hide why an action is unavailable when the reason affects user trust, such as checkout not being available here, an expired cart, out-of-date business data, a required handoff, or an unsupported business.

Internal-to-user translation rule:

| Internal condition                   | User-facing meaning                                         |
| ------------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------- |
| `partner_required`                   | This store is not ready on Arro yet.                        |
| `denied` or revoked access           | Arro cannot shop directly with this store right now.        |
| `rate_limited` or connector overload | This store is temporarily busy.                             |
| `error`, stale profile, schema issue | Store data is temporarily unavailable or out of date.       |
| Low internal match quality           | Arro could not find a reliable match from supported stores. |

Never show the internal term, protocol field, raw score, threshold, exception name, model name, or error code in user-facing UI, notifications, share cards, or agent summaries.

### Integration-Scoped Session and Optional Account Migration

Core discovery and comparison work in V1 happens before permanent Arro account creation.

Required behavior:

- Use one session model across authorized agents, MCP tools, HTTP integrations, business integrations, connectors that need user/task context, and future first-party surfaces. The session model is integration-scoped and tied to Arro's unified schema, policy, audit, and memory boundaries.
- Allow search, product detail lookup, comparison, source-state inspection, typed memory, and cart-preparation readiness through an integration-scoped agent session without creating a permanent Arro account.

- Preserve server-side guest or agent context only where needed for active search, comparison, rate limits, cart handoff, checkout handoff, typed memory, or explicit account migration.

### Unsupported Business Fallback

If a business or ecommerce site is not supported, Arro still helps the user move forward within approved fallback limits.

Required behavior:

- Extract likely product signals such as title, brand, model, category, color, size, barcode, SKU, or visible attributes.

- Explain in plain language why Arro believes a result is relevant, and avoid claiming exact equivalence unless identifiers or supported source lookup confirm it.

It may submit only user-approved visible current-page product signals, selected screenshots, selected text, barcode/product identifiers, and explicit share-to-compare inputs through Arro APIs. ### Product Sanity-Check Tool Result

The product sanity-check flow turns the strongest old consumer-utility ideas into a headless V1 value pattern. It lets agents and integrations ask "is this product, claim, deal, variant, or checkout path safe enough to continue?" without launching a first-party consumer surface.

Required behavior:

- Say "do not buy," "wait," "handoff only," or "source cannot verify this" when evidence is weak, stale, unsupported, or outside the user's explicit constraints.
- Preserve accepted alternatives, rejected alternatives, user corrections, no-buy warning reasons, and stale references as typed decision state when the session or integration is allowed to store them.
- Route to cart preparation, checkout handoff, or gated `complete_checkout` only after the normal source-governance, `ActionPolicy`, user authorization, server-bound idempotency, rate-limit, revalidation, and payment-handler gates pass.
- Measure value through bad alternatives rejected, unsupported claims blocked, wrong variants prevented, stale prices corrected, return-risk warnings shown, source gaps detected, and user-confirmed corrections, reviewed for precision and balanced against safe completions, useful handoffs, and user satisfaction.

### One Prompt Checkout Preparation Experience

In the broader V1 envelope, One Prompt Checkout means one-prompt checkout preparation: plan assembly, cart preparation, checkout handoff, and gated checkout completion only where every completion gate passes. Required behavior:

### Owned Arro Commerce AI and Super-App Surfaces (Post-V1)

Arro-owned AI and super-app surfaces are accepted post-V1 product directions, not new V1 scope. They are distribution and experience layers on top of the headless core, not replacements for source governance, UCP authority, MCP/API contracts, or merchant-controlled checkout.

The user value of owned AI is not "another chatbot." It is a commerce-specialized operator that remembers shopping intent with permission, keeps decision context alive, catches stale or risky purchases, compares across supported sources, prepares business-scoped carts, uses gated `complete_checkout` only where completion gates pass, and continues post-purchase support where source capabilities allow it.

Required behavior:

- Use the same HTTP API, Arro MCP tools, `IntentRecord` / `DecisionReceipt`, `ActionPolicy`, Source Liquidity Map, Intent Liquidity Map, commercial-isolation rules, auth, and audit services as external agents.
- Keep the assistant commerce-specialized: shopping research, comparison, saved decisions, watches, no-buy warnings, cart preparation, gated `complete_checkout`, order context, returns/support handoff, and lifecycle utility.
- Do not turn the owned assistant into a generic life/work automation agent, merchant dashboard, ad surface, crawler, wallet, direct connector caller, marketplace seller, or universal checkout owner.
- Make every recommendation, warning, action, handoff, completion, watch, and lifecycle message source-labeled, resumable, revocable where private, and auditable.
- Prefer message-native and task-native UX patterns where they reduce friction, but keep sensitive actions gated by explicit confirmation, step-up authorization, final revalidation, and plain state summaries.
- Let owned surfaces benefit from Arro's typed commerce memory only after explicit user consent. Do not infer private preferences from inboxes, camera rolls, browsing history, or third-party accounts unless a later scope review approves a specific source, consent model, and retention policy.
- Treat visual capture, screenshots, saved images, social references, and share-to-shop inputs as user-submitted evidence only until supported source lookup or cart/checkout revalidation confirms commerce facts.
- Express care through protection, clarity, continuity, and timely restraint: useful memory, plain uncertainty, no-buy warnings, prepared handoff recovery, and safe next actions. Do not use friendliness, urgency, social pressure, or over-personified warmth to increase reliance when evidence is weak, source authority is missing, or checkout gates fail.
- Keep no-buy value visible. A first-party assistant should feel trustworthy because it can say "wait," "not enough evidence," "wrong variant risk," or "use merchant handoff" instead of forcing conversion.
- Measure owned AI by trust outcomes, repeat-use continuity, saved re-entry work, no-buy prevention, source-gap reduction, and successful safe handoffs or gated completions, not only GMV or conversion.

## 7. Protocol, API, MCP, and Channel Flow

Arro's V1 interoperability strategy is one headless commerce core with two controlled front doors: the HTTP API and the Arro MCP server. Future browser extension, messaging bot, first-party app, and external channel surfaces must call the same source governance, product intelligence, policy, connector, and audit services.

Required flow:

```text
External agent, integration, or future first-party surface
  -> Arro HTTP API or Arro MCP server
  -> API Plane and Agentic Commerce Plane
  -> IntentRecord / DecisionReceipt, ActionPolicy, Source Liquidity Map
```

## 10. Launch Summary

MLP launch scope: headless UCP-native shopping intelligence and policy-bounded commerce preflight plus one narrow execution proof exposed through the Arro HTTP API and one canonical Arro MCP server. The MLP proof is a real external-agent call path with verified discovery, source-labeled product detail, simple comparison, product sanity-check receipts, minimal `IntentRecord` / `DecisionReceipt`, read/compare/limited/denied/blocked/checkout-continuation/gated-completion `ActionPolicy`, limited source-liquidity evidence, prepared `continue_url` fallback, one business-scoped gated `complete_checkout` path, minimal retention and data-rights handling for MLP session, receipt, continuation, and attempt records, a preview Commerce Capability Manifest, a preview Agent Distribution Kit, Agent Harness replay evidence, documentation-led first-success integration, familiar-commerce output shapes, human-readable receipt or no-buy rendering, agent task-success metrics, no-buy and what-could-go-wrong value metrics, browser-agent preflight guidance, affiliate-attribution isolation where safe, commercial parity, and merchant representation controls. Gift intent support, strict typed commerce memory beyond scoped receipt/session references, typed decision artifacts, role-scoped multi-agent workflow support, multi-business planning beyond the pilot, broad cart preparation, broad checkout handoff, broad gated checkout completion, secondhand/resale breadth, coupons, checkout quotes beyond the pilot, order references, post-purchase support context, payment-handler readiness metadata beyond the pilot gate, full Agent Distribution Kit, and broad managed-channel adapters are post-MLP V1 expansion features and become launch-visible only where approved sources, negotiated capabilities, and their own gates pass.

Unsupported, limited, denied, or partner-required businesses are normal operating states. The product must degrade clearly while staying provider-independent. HTTP APIs and MCP are the V1 controlled front doors into the same Arro commerce core; owned commerce AI, commerce super-app shells, messaging, OS-assistant, wearable, spatial, smart-glasses, social, browser-extension, and first-party web/mobile surfaces are post-V1 consumers of that same core, not new merchant-authority layers. Multi-business shopping is a planning, handoff, and per-business completion layer, not a universal checkout. Unbounded autonomous commerce remains out of V1; gated completion requires UCP-first business control, explicit user authorization, payment-handler safety, rate limits, privacy review, regulatory review, and complete auditability.
